Hello! I am a Ph.D. student in Political Science at the University of Minnesota. My research focuses on international relations and political economy, with particular interests in strategic bargaining, credible commitments, and bureaucratic accountability.

My research agenda has two primary focuses. First, I examine how strategic interdependence shapes the credibility of threats and commitments in international bargaining. I am particularly interested in how states’ own interests constrain their ability to extract concessions: states that provide protection or other benefits may find it difficult to withdraw them when doing so would undermine their own interests.

Second, I examine the political economy of bureaucratic accountability and public goods provision. I study how higher-level authorities’ ability to observe and verify performance shapes bureaucratic incentives and the spatial allocation of public resources. This research asks when stronger accountability improves public goods provision and when it instead redirects resources toward activities and locations that are easier to verify.

My other work examines public opinion on international order and foreign policy, including perceptions of global justice and preferences concerning relations with the West and the Global South. Across these areas, I draw on rational-choice theory and quantitative methods, with a broader interest in how information and institutional arrangements shape political incentives and constrain the exercise of power.
